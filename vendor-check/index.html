Vendor Check

Free supplier checks for people starting a lash or hair-extension business. Each check below takes a few minutes, and each claim links to its source. Nothing here is a recommendation of any supplier, and we have not ordered from any of them.

1. "Gold Supplier" is a paid membership, not an audit

A Gold Supplier badge means the seller pays for an Alibaba membership. Sourcing guides describe the check behind it as basic business-licence verification, not a visit to the factory. Treat it as "this seller pays to be here", nothing more.

Sources: Cosmo Sourcing, Cosmo Sourcing, "Is Alibaba legit?"

2. "Verified Supplier" comes with a report. Read it

Alibaba says a Verified Supplier has passed a third-party inspection covering its business licence, facilities, quality control and R&D, and that a capability report is published on the storefront. The audit is a point-in-time check of the company and does not test the products it makes. Open that report and check that the factory address and product lines match what you are buying (lash trays, not general cosmetics).

Source: Alibaba.com seller site, Verified Supplier

3. Trade Assurance only protects money paid on Alibaba

Alibaba's Trade Assurance covers payment, delivery and product quality on orders you place and pay for on Alibaba.com. If a seller asks you to pay by bank transfer, PayPal or WhatsApp invoice outside the platform, that order has no Trade Assurance. Check the "Protection with Trade Assurance" box on the product page before you pay.

Sources: Alibaba.com, "Stay on platform, stay protected", Guided Imports

4. Compare years on Alibaba with the revenue it claims

A storefront shows how many years it has been on Alibaba and a revenue band. One lash storefront we checked showed 1 year on the platform next to a $10M–$50M revenue band. That is not proof of anything, but it is a question to ask before you order: "Which factory makes your trays, and since when?"

Source: our lash vendor directory, checked 2026-09-19 against the live storefront.

5. Look up the age of the seller's own website

If the supplier has its own domain, look it up on ICANN Lookup and read the creation date. A website older than the Alibaba storefront is a mild sign the business existed before it started selling there. A domain registered last month for a "10-year factory" is worth a question.

Source: ICANN Registration Data Lookup

6. "No scam reports found" means nothing was found

Search the company's exact registered name with words like "scam", "complaint" and "review". If nothing comes up, that is all it means: a new or small seller can simply have no reviews yet. Watch for name collisions: a complaint about "Love Hair" is not a complaint about "Lody Love Hair".

7. Before the sample order: a checklist

  • Order the exact style you plan to sell (curl, length, band), not the seller's best sample.
  • Pay on the platform, so the sample has Trade Assurance (check 3).
  • Ask for the MOQ, unit price at that MOQ, and lead time in writing in the chat.
  • When it arrives, photograph it next to the listing photos before you use it.
  • Ask whether your bulk order ships from the same factory as the sample.
